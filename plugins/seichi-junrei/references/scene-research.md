# Find the places a fan would otherwise walk past

Use this when the value of the trip is discovery: a familiar work makes an ordinary place meaningful. The output is a defensible scene/place connection and an enjoyable observation, not a longer list of tourist attractions. A legitimate inconclusive result is useful; a confident invented pin is not.

## Discover, then identify

1. Fix the adaptation, edition and relevant sequence. Search in the work's language and the destination's local language/English, using character actions, food, signage, architecture and nearby places as well as the title. Search a distinctive branch or detail once a lead appears; do not keep repeating broad top-ten queries.
2. Use production interviews, location scouting accounts, fan comparison reports and local residents' dated visits as leads. Fan research is valuable, particularly for incidental backgrounds. Open the original account: lists can copy one guess repeatedly. Multiple URLs do not establish independent corroboration.
3. Split each lead into **what happens in the work**, **which physical place matches**, and **what can be visited now**. A venue directory confirms an address, not that the anime depicts it. A production interview confirming a city does not confirm every restaurant in it. Promotional murals and fan exhibits are related attractions, not in-story locations.
4. Where lawful material is available, compare the actual frame or passage with real imagery. Use distinctive geometry: roof shape, window arrangement, canopy supports, street bend, neighboring facades, relative position and viewing direction. Check a plausible alternative, especially for repeated streetscapes and chain outlets. One generic architectural style or plate colour is weak evidence by itself. Note mirrored, compressed or composite backgrounds.
5. Inspect the pixels. An image labelled "film still" may actually be an old map screenshot; filenames, alt text and search summaries can misdescribe it. Record which images were actually viewed, their source and date. Keep research screenshots private unless their redistribution is permitted. Do not add copyrighted frames to the public sample as a substitute for research.
6. Verify present use through the operator, municipality or an appropriately dated local source. Check relocation, refurbishment, renamed businesses, closures, visitor boundaries and photography access. Do not infer the exact table, menu, facade or room survives because a business still has the same name. Read the actual branch/address block: search snippets can merge opening hours from adjacent outlets. Do not carry old opening hours into the route. If current sources conflict, report that conflict rather than selecting a convenient value.

## Keep a compact claim ledger

For each material stop, record:

| Field | What changes the decision |
| --- | --- |
| Scene | Adaptation plus episode/chapter/sequence and action; use a verified locator or omit invented precision. |
| Place | Name, branch/address, search area or exact observation point; distinguish these levels. |
| Story evidence | Source and whether it was directly inspected, author-reported or merely repeated. |
| Physical clues | Observed matching features, viewing direction and plausible competing location. |
| Contradictions | Missing/changed features, author hedges and contrary evidence. |
| Current use | Source, check date, renovation/closure/access limits and remaining unknowns. |
| Decision | Verified, documented candidate, unresolved, related attraction, or excluded. Explain the reason. |
| Fan experience | What to notice, compare, eat or recognize at this specific place, in original prose. |
| Next useful check | The unresolved claim that would change inclusion; omit checks that would not. |

Use connection grades from `research-and-ranking.md` separately from the decision. A documented candidate is not creator-confirmed inspiration. No automatic numeric score upgrades missing evidence.

## Admission and stopping rules

- Promote a point to **verified** only when the available evidence supports that specific scene/place claim. Say whether verification is direct comparison or explicit authoritative confirmation; do not imply both.
- A credible first-hand comparison can remain a **documented candidate** without a production endorsement. Explain the basis and limitation if it is shown to the user. Missing direct footage access is not a reason to fabricate a timestamp or silently call the stop verified.
- Preserve originating hedges such as "predicted", "looks similar" and "not certain". Later unqualified repetition does not erase them. Exclude an unresolved guess from a confirmed route.
- Do not demand a fixed number of sources, stops or visual features. Continue only for a named unresolved claim. Once the needed match and visit conditions are supported, stop equivalent searching. If an important check is unavailable, mark the gap and continue independent research; request the smallest missing input only when necessary.
- Distinguish a finished research note, a user-approved selection, a rendered guide and a published sample. A research case must not carry fixture consent into a new trip.

## Turn discovery into the guide

Write the chapter around the scene and its physical clue. For example: identify the breakfast branch, notice the background roof and then enjoy breakfast there. This is stronger than attaching character art to a generic food recommendation. Keep a famous landmark when it helps orient the journey; do not let it crowd out the discoveries the user asked for.

Read `../examples/conan_singapore_research.md` for an incomplete but inspected research case, including why an attractive street lead was withheld. Treat its dates and access findings as historical evidence requiring refresh for a later trip.
