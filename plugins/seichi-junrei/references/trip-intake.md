# Understand the trip before production

Read this at the start of a new trip, or when resuming without a usable trip brief. The aim is to understand the planning stage and material constraints before committing to a route, image batch or finished booklet. Do not turn intake into a long mandatory questionnaire or another approval ceremony.

## Read first, ask only for what is missing

Accept ordinary text, a supplied itinerary/export, connected calendar events, or a combination. Read the user's existing conversation and supplied sources before asking them to repeat details. For calendar access, use the bounded-date and privacy rules in `calendar-and-privacy.md`; a connected calendar is optional, not required. If it is unavailable, continue from text/export and disclose the specific missing source.

Classify the trip in practical terms:

| Situation | Planning approach |
| --- | --- |
| Mostly decided | Preserve confirmed flights, lodging, fixed appointments and the user's chosen order. Build pilgrimage visits into genuinely usable windows, including transfers, check-in, meals, rest and return travel. Identify conflicts instead of silently moving reservations. |
| Starting from scratch | Establish destination/date flexibility, rough duration, priorities and relevant constraints first. Research suitable works and compare route concepts before detailed image production. Proposed cities, lodging areas and transport remain proposals; no reservation is implied. |
| Partly decided | Keep the fixed parts as anchors and develop the open parts around them. For example, booked flights and an undecided hotel do not make the flights tentative or the hotel confirmed. |

These are planning modes, not rigid categories. Maintain status per item: **confirmed by the user/reservation**, **tentative**, **suggested by the agent**, **unknown**, or **cancelled**. Note the supporting source and last relevant update privately. Calendar presence and a calendar provider's event status do not alone prove a travel booking. Accept the user's explicit statement that something is booked; do not demand a ticket merely to reconfirm it. If sources conflict, surface that specific conflict without overwriting the user's stated decision.

## Build one concise trip brief

Collect what affects this task, using existing evidence whenever possible:

- Dates or date flexibility, trip length, destinations and intended travel order.
- Flights, accommodation, fixed commitments and usable free time, with each item's status. A hotel name or public area can inform travel feasibility without exposing private booking details.
- Party and material constraints: who the guide serves, mobility/walking tolerance, travel pace, budget range and relevant transport preferences. Ask only where the answer changes the proposal.
- Interests: selected work(s), familiar genres/characters, discovery versus famous landmarks, detour tolerance and spoiler preference.
- Intended use: personal/companion travel or business/customer/promotion. Apply the whole-booklet policy in `rights-and-use.md`; free customer handouts can still be business use. Do not ask again when the purpose is already known.
- This pass's deliverable: destination/work candidates, a tentative route, or a usable finished booklet; intended private/group/public sharing and whether dates/identities should be dummy model data.
- Remaining assumptions and unresolved decisions, identifying which ones could change the route, artwork or page structure.

Ask one short grouped round for material gaps rather than discovering them one at a time during production. Do not ask every bullet by default. Summarize the understood fixed parts, open parts and intended deliverable in ordinary language. Existing clear instructions authorize continuing; do not require a ritual confirmation when nothing material remains undecided. A real unresolved choice stays open until answered or explicitly delegated.

## Match production effort to readiness

All bookings need not be complete. The question is whether enough is known for the **requested stage**:

- Candidate discovery can proceed from a city/region, rough window and interests. Compare evidence and feasibility cheaply; do not manufacture a final day-by-day booklet yet.
- A tentative route can use clearly labelled assumptions, approximate lodging areas and alternatives. Resolve the main route choice before commissioning a full illustration set.
- A finished trip booklet needs the selected work, route/day allocation, relevant fixed anchors, practical constraints and the visual storyboard. An unknown that would materially change these should be clarified before expensive image generation or full layout. Minor details can remain visibly approximate when appropriate; do not invent hotel names, times, bookings or precise starts to make a plan look complete.
- A user-requested public model/demo can deliberately use agreed dummy dates, representative public locations and model transport assumptions. Label them; do not demand personal bookings for a demonstration.

For example, an exact hotel is not needed to compare Singapore work candidates. It matters before promising a tightly timed walk starting at that hotel. Likewise, a late arrival is not automatically a free sightseeing evening. Work selection still follows the existing user-choice rule; retain choices already made instead of asking again.

## Keep changes local

Save the private brief and decisions in the trip workspace, outside public fixtures. Before changing an agreed route, show the changed assumption and its consequences. Update only the affected route, text, maps or artwork; preserve approved unaffected material. A hotel change may require recalculating the first/last leg, not regenerating every character illustration. Reservations and calendar changes are separate actions from planning; this intake does not authorize making or changing them.

## Natural-language starting requests

These are examples, not a required input format:

> Read the travel events in my connected calendar for my Singapore trip on [dates]. Flights and hotel are booked. Find pilgrimage options that fit the free time; keep existing reservations fixed. First summarize the conditions and ask for important gaps together.

> I am considering about five days in Europe, but dates and hotels are undecided. I like MONSTER. Start by understanding my constraints and proposing routes; do not begin the full booklet or image batch until the main direction is settled.

> The flights are booked, but accommodation and sightseeing are open. Use the flight times I wrote below as fixed anchors. Help decide the rest, distinguish suggestions from bookings, then make the guide once the route and conditions are clear.
